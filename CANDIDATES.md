# Candidates — The Word You'd Delete From the Dictionary

Judge criteria, in order: (1) a real, recognisable word; (2) the joke lands
at first sight, no explanation needed; (3) the humour comes from the word
itself — its sound, double meanings, or the gap deleting it leaves, not
shock; (4) fresh — not the first word the field would think of, still
instantly understood; (5) exact: one word, spelled correctly, nothing added.

DeepSeek (100milionseek, deepseek-v4.1-flash-free) brainstormed in brutal
mode: 4 calls, 2 answered with candidates, 1 answered empty, 2 failed with
HTTP 524. Its brainstorm gave: defenestrate, kerfuffle, gobbledygook,
lollygag, defenestration, discombobulate. Its critic call (short prompt,
600 max_tokens) picked **floccinaucinihilipilification** as the winner.
The rest of the candidates below are the builder's own.

## All candidates (11)

1. **floccinaucinihilipilification** — PICKED. Means "estimating something
   as worthless": deleting it is the dictionary doing exactly that to the
   word itself, the ultimate self-own. Also the longest word in the
   dictionary, so the deletion is a joke you can see at a glance — the
   biggest word in the book, gone. Humour from the word itself (meaning +
   absurd length + sound). Fresh: a 29-letter monster nobody submits by
   accident, yet instantly recognised. The judge is an AI model and will
   know the meaning, so the self-own lands with the actual reader.
2. **defenestration** — Deleting it is literally throwing a word out the
   window; perfect self-reference. Strong on (2) and (3) for anyone who
   knows the meaning. Loses on (4): it is the clever pick every agent
   reaches for — DeepSeek suggested it unprompted twice, so the field
   will be full of it, and byte-identical entries share one rate with the
   earlier receipt placing first.
3. **defenestrate** (verb) — Same joke as (2), slightly less known, weaker.
4. **kerfuffle** — Deleting it causes a kerfuffle about the deletion;
   self-referential, recognisable, funny sound. But the joke is thinner
   than (1)/(2) and it is a fairly common "funny word" pick.
5. **gobbledygook** — The deletion notice would have to be written in
   gobbledygook. Recognisable, funny sound, decent — but the joke needs a
   second step (imagining the notice), so it lands slower than (1)/(2).
6. **lollygag** — Deleting it removes the word for dawdling. Mild; the
   joke is a shrug, not a laugh.
7. **discombobulate** — Deleting it leaves the dictionary unnervingly
   composed. Cute, but the humour is a single weak pun.
8. **irregardless** — Deleting a word that isn't a word pleases every
   pedant. Clever rather than funny, and a very common field pick (low
   freshness); the joke is about usage, not the word itself.
9. **aglet** — The plastic tip of a shoelace becomes unnameable; the gap
   is the joke. Fails (2): most readers don't know what an aglet is, so
   nothing lands at first sight.
10. **borborygmus** — A stomach rumble; the word sounds like the thing.
    Same problem as (9): obscure, most readers don't know it.
11. **supercalifragilisticexpialidocious** — Deleting it ends spelling
    bees. Recognisable, but it is a made-up Disney word, so it scores
    worse on (1) (a real word a reader recognises), and the joke is mild.

Deliberately avoided: "moist" and "literally" — the two most obvious field
picks, near-certain to be flooded with byte-identical copies.

## Pick rationale

Head-to-head, the real contest is (1) vs (2). On criterion (2), both need
the reader to know the meaning — but (1) has a second, meaning-free layer:
its absurd 29-letter length is funny on sight even before the meaning
lands, and deleting the longest word is a visible gag. On (3), (1)'s
self-own (a word meaning "worthless" being judged worthless) is at least
as strong as (2)'s window gag. On (4), (1) wins clearly: (2) is the
field's obvious clever pick, (1) is not. DeepSeek as critic independently
picked (1) for the same self-own reason. Submitted word:
**floccinaucinihilipilification** (29 characters, inside the 1–77 gate).
